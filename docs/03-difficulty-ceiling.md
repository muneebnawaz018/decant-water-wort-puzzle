# The difficulty ceiling

An open problem in Decant, written so someone outside the project can pick it
up. Background is in `docs/01-water-sort.md` (game spec) and
`docs/00-overview.md` (what the app is).

## The problem in one sentence

Past level 500 every board is generated from the same difficulty spec, so
level 501 and level 5000 are the same puzzle with a different random seed — and
we have run out of the levers the current formula knows how to pull.

## What the game is, for anyone new

Colored liquid sits in a column of glass vials. Tapping a source vial and then
a destination pours the **whole top run** of matching segments, capped by the
free space in the destination. A level is solved when every vial holds one
color or is empty. There is no timer and no fail state.

The three numbers that describe a board:

- **color count** — how many distinct liquids, each with exactly `capacity`
  segments in play
- **capacity** — segments per vial
- **spare vials** — empty vials above the minimum, the working space

Every board is generated by reverse-generation: start from the solved state,
apply `scrambleSteps` random legal un-pours, then check the result against an
acceptance gate. Generation is seeded, so level N is byte-identical on every
device and no board is ever stored.

## Where the curve is now

`src/game/levelParams.ts` holds an eight-row table. The last row is open-ended:

```text
maxLevel     colors  capacity  spare  scramble
5            3        4         2      8
20           4        4         2      16
50           5        4         2      28
100          6        4         2      40
200          7        4         2      55
350          8        4         1      70
500          9        5         1      90
Infinity     10-12    5         1      110
```

Three difficulty modes then adjust that row: Easy adds a spare vial and scrambles
less, Hard adds a color, removes a spare vial and scrambles more.

So a level past 500 is: 10 to 12 colors, capacity 5, one spare vial, 110
scramble steps. Forever.

## Why each existing lever is finished

**Colors are capped at 12.** Not by the code — by human vision. The palette
already fails under simulated protanopia and deuteranopia at full board size
(closest pair under dE 1), which is why colorblind glyphs exist and why a test
asserts the failure rather than pretending otherwise. A thirteenth color makes
the accessibility story worse without making the puzzle deeper.

**Spare vials are floored at one.** Zero spare vials is not a hard puzzle, it is
an unsolvable one in most positions. The spec calls spare vials the strongest
difficulty lever there is, and it has been spent.

**Scramble steps stopped meaning anything around 60.** This is the one worth
saying loudly, because it looks like a free lever and is not: a random walk over
board states converges to its stationary distribution. Past that point more
steps do not produce a more mixed board, they produce another draw from the same
distribution. 110 steps and 300 steps generate the same difficulty spread. The
number can be raised; nothing happens except slower generation.

## What "harder" should even mean here

Worth stating, because the obvious answers are wrong.

Not "more colors" — that is more bookkeeping, not more thinking.

Not "more moves" — a board can need 50 moves and demand no planning at all if
every move is forced.

The thing that makes a water sort board hard is **how far ahead you have to see
before committing to a pour**. That is: how many legal moves lead to positions
you must undo, how deep the shortest solution is relative to how deep a greedy
player would go, and how much of the board's state is hidden from a player who
only looks one move ahead.

Any proposal should be measured against that, not against a bigger number in a
table.

## Directions considered

Listed with what we already know about each. None are decided.

### Capacity as a continuous lever

Deeper vials bury information: at capacity 4 you can see every segment on the
board, at 6 the lower segments are unreachable until you dig. `waterCore` is
already capacity-agnostic and the renderer derives segment height from capacity,
so this is mostly a table change plus checking the board still fits the screen.

Open question: where does it stop being harder and start being tedious? Nobody
has playtested capacity 6.

### Gate on the solution, not the setup

We already compute true optimal solution length — `optimalMoves` in
`src/core/solver.ts`, an IDA\* search used for star ratings. Generation currently
accepts a board on a fragmentation heuristic. It could instead reject boards
whose optimal solution is too shallow for the level, scaling the requirement
upward.

That measures the thing we actually care about. The catch is cost: median 1ms
but p95 11ms and worst case 133ms on a development machine, and rejection
sampling multiplies that by however many boards get thrown away. Not viable at
level-load time on a phone.

The way around it is to move generation offline — search once on a workstation,
ship a table of accepted seeds. That trades a runtime cost for a build step and
a small data file, and it breaks the current property that any level number can
be generated on demand.

### New mechanics

When arithmetic runs out, the genre's usual answers are hidden segments (lower
segments revealed only as they are uncovered), locked vials (unusable until N
colors are complete), mixed capacities on one board, and segments that take two
pours to move.

Each of these is a real feature: the core, the solver, the generator and the
renderer all have to learn it. They are not curve edits.

### Something better

The above is what we thought of. The interesting answers are probably the ones
that measure a board's difficulty directly — deadlock proximity, branching
factor at each decision point, how badly a greedy solver underperforms optimal —
and use that measurement as the acceptance gate. That space is open.

## The hard constraint on any answer

**Determinism is part of the save format.** No board is stored anywhere. Level N
in a given mode is rebuilt from `seedForLevel(level, DIFFICULTY_SALT[mode])`
every time it is opened, which means the difficulty curve, the salts, the
generator and the RNG are all load-bearing. Change any of them and every
player's saved progress silently points at different puzzles — same level
numbers, different boards, and a saved half-finished level that no longer
replays.

`src/game/__tests__/difficulty.test.ts` pins level 30 against a recorded
fingerprint to catch exactly this. When it fails, the fix is a migration, not a
re-recorded expectation.

So a proposal that changes levels 1 to 500 needs a versioning story: keep the
old curve for records written before the change, or migrate them deliberately.
A proposal that only adds rows beyond 500 is much cheaper, because nothing has
been played there yet.
